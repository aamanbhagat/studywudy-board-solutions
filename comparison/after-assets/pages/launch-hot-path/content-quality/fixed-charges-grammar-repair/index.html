Two charges –q each are fixed separated by distance 2d. A third charge q of mass m placed at the mid-point is displaced slightly by x(x << d) perpendicular to the line joining the two fixed charges as shown in figure. Show that q will perform simple harmonic oscillation of time period.

T = [(8pi3 ε_0 md3)/q^2]^(1/2)

Step-by-step solution

Setting up the geometry

Place the origin at the midpoint. The two fixed charges −q are at (−d,0) and (+d,0). The mobile charge +q (mass m) is displaced to (0,x) with x≪d.

Step 1 – Distance from each fixed charge to the displaced position

By Pythagoras, each fixed charge is at distance
r=d2+x2
from the displaced position of +q.

Step 2 – Coulomb force magnitude from each −q

Since the interaction is attractive:
F=14πε0·q2d2+x2

Step 3 – Resolving forces; net restoring force

By symmetry the horizontal (along the original line) components of the two forces cancel. Each force has a vertical component (anti-parallel to x):
sinθ=xd2+x2
The total restoring force is
Fnet=−2Fsinθ=−2q24πε0·x(d2+x2)3/2

Step 4 – Small-displacement approximation (x≪d)

(d2+x2)3/2≈d3
∴Fnet≈−q22πε0d3x

This is of the form F=−kx with k=q22πε0d3, confirming SHM.

Step 5 – Angular frequency

Using Newton's second law for SHM, Fnet=−mω2x:
mω2=q22πε0d3⇒ω=q22πε0md3

Step 6 – Time period

T=2πω=2π2πε0md3q2

Squaring:
T2=4π2·2πε0md3q2=8π3ε0md3q2

Therefore:
T=[8π3ε0md3q2]1/2

  1. Step 1

    Geometry – distance to displaced charge

    The origin is at the midpoint. Each fixed –q is at horizontal distance d. When +q moves perpendicularly by x, its distance from each fixed charge becomes r = √(d² + x²).

    r=d2+x2

  2. Step 2

    Coulomb force magnitude from each –q

    The interaction between +q and each –q is attractive. By Coulomb's law the magnitude of each force is F = q²/[4πε₀(d²+x²)].

    F=q24πε0(d2+x2)

  3. Step 3

    Net restoring force by symmetry

    The two horizontal (x-axis) components are equal and opposite and cancel exactly. The two vertical (y-axis) components are both directed toward the original equilibrium (restoring). Each makes angle θ with the horizontal where sin θ = x/√(d²+x²). The total restoring force is Fnet = –2F sin θ.

    Fnet=−2q24πε0·x(d2+x2)3/2

  4. Step 4

    Small-displacement approximation

    Since x ≪ d, we have (d²+x²)^{3/2} ≈ d³. The restoring force simplifies to Fnet ≈ –[q²/(2πε₀d³)]x, which is linear in x, proving SHM.

    Fnet≈−q22πε0d3x

  5. Step 5

    Spring constant and angular frequency

    Comparing with F = –mω²x gives the effective spring constant k = q²/(2πε₀d³). Hence ω² = k/m.

    ω2=q22πε0md3

  6. Step 6

    Time period of SHM

    T = 2π/ω. Squaring T² = 4π²/ω² = 4π² × 2πε₀md³/q² = 8π³ε₀md³/q², giving the required result.

    T=[8π3ε0md3q2]1/2

Final answer

The charge +q performs SHM with time period T=[8π3ε0md3q2]1/2 The restoring force arises because the net attractive force from the two fixed –q charges has a component anti-parallel to the displacement x, and for x ≪ d this force is proportional to x.

Additional study notes

Understand the answer

Choose the midpoint of the fixed charges as the origin. Thus, the two charges −q are at (−d, 0) and (+d, 0), while the movable charge +q is at (0, x) after a small perpendicular displacement. Its distance from either fixed charge is r = √(d2 + x2).

Each fixed charge attracts +q with magnitude F = q2/[4πε0(d2 + x2)]. The two forces are inclined symmetrically. Their components parallel to the line joining the fixed charges are equal and opposite, so they cancel. Notice that these individual components do not vanish; only their resultant does. The perpendicular components point towards the original midpoint and therefore add. For each force, the relevant component factor is sin θ = x/√(d2 + x2). Hence,

F_net = −2F sin θ = −[2q2/(4πε0)]x/(d2 + x2)^(3/2).

The minus sign is essential: it shows that the resultant force is opposite to the displacement. If the charge is moved to the other side, x changes sign and the force reverses as well, always directing the particle back towards equilibrium.

This exact force is not strictly proportional to x because its denominator also depends on x. However, the question specifies x ≪ d. Therefore, x2 is negligible compared with d2 and (d2 + x2)^(3/2) ≈ d3. The force then becomes

F_net ≈ −[q2/(2πε0d3)]x.

This has the defining SHM form F = −kx, with effective force constant k = q2/(2πε0d3). Comparing it with F = −mω2x gives

ω2 = q2/(2πε0md3).

Using T = 2π/ω,

T = 2π√(2πε0md3/q2) = √(8π3ε0md3/q2).

Two checks confirm the result. First, the restoring force becomes zero at x = 0, as required at equilibrium. Second, the final expression shows that increasing the mass makes the oscillation slower, whereas the strength of the electrical interaction enters through q2. The motion is simple harmonic only under the stated small-displacement approximation; the exact force law is non-linear for larger x.

Reasoning path

  1. Set the fixed charges at (−d, 0) and (+d, 0), and the displaced movable charge at (0, x).
  2. Use Pythagoras to obtain the equal separation r = √(d2 + x2).
  3. Resolve both attractive Coulomb forces: the components along the fixed-charge line cancel, while the perpendicular restoring components add.
  4. Write the exact resultant as F_net = −[2q2/(4πε0)]x/(d2 + x2)^(3/2).
  5. Apply x ≪ d to replace (d2 + x2)^(3/2) by d3, obtaining a force proportional to −x.
  6. Compare with −mω2x and use T = 2π/ω to obtain T = [8π3ε0md3/q2]^(1/2).

Common mistake

A common error is to add the full magnitudes of the two Coulomb forces. Only their perpendicular components add; the components along the line joining the fixed charges cancel. Another error is to claim exact SHM without applying x ≪ d, since the unapproximated denominator depends on x.

Exam tip

First derive the exact vector component of the resultant force, and only then use the small-displacement approximation. Explicitly retain the negative sign to demonstrate that the force is restoring before comparing it with F = −mω2x.
