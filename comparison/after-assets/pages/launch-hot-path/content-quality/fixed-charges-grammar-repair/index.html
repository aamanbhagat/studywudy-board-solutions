Two charges –q each are fixed separated by distance 2d. A third charge q of mass m placed at the mid-point is displaced slightly by x(x << d) perpendicular to the line joining the two fixed charges as shown in figure. Show that q will perform simple harmonic oscillation of time period.

T = [(8pi^3 ε_0 md^3)/q^2]^(1/2)

Step-by-step solution

Answer

Setting up the geometry

Place the origin at the midpoint. The two fixed charges −q are at (−d,0) and (+d,0). The mobile charge +q (mass m) is displaced to (0,x) with xlld.

Step 1 – Distance from each fixed charge to the displaced position

By Pythagoras, each fixed charge is at distance
r=d2+x2
from the displaced position of +q.

Step 2 – Coulomb force magnitude from each −q

Since the interaction is attractive:
F=14πε0·q2d2+x2

Step 3 – Resolving forces; net restoring force

By symmetry the horizontal (along the original line) components of the two forces cancel. Each force has a vertical component (anti-parallel to x):
sinθ=xd2+x2
The total restoring force is
Fnet=−2Fsinθ=−2q24πε0·x(d2+x2)3/2

Step 4 – Small-displacement approximation (xlld)

(d2+x2)3/2≈d3
therefore Fnet≈−q22πε0d3x

This is of the form F=−kx with k=q22πε0d3, confirming SHM.

Step 5 – Angular frequency

Using Newton's second law for SHM, Fnet=−mω2x:
mω2=q22πε0d3⇒ω=q22πε0md3

Step 6 – Time period

T=2πω=2π2πε0md3q2

Squaring:
T2=4π2·2πε0md3q2=8π3ε0md3q2

Therefore:
T=[8π3ε0md3q2]1/2

Step-by-step solution

  1. Step 1

    Geometry – distance to displaced charge

    The origin is at the midpoint. Each fixed –q is at horizontal distance d. When +q moves perpendicularly by x, its distance from each fixed charge becomes r = √(d² + x²).

    r=d2+x2

  2. Step 2

    Coulomb force magnitude from each –q

    The interaction between +q and each –q is attractive. By Coulomb's law the magnitude of each force is F = q²/[4πε₀(d²+x²)].

    F=q24πε0(d2+x2)

  3. Step 3

    Net restoring force by symmetry

    The two horizontal (x-axis) components are equal and opposite and cancel exactly. The two vertical (y-axis) components are both directed toward the original equilibrium (restoring). Each makes angle θ with the horizontal where sin θ = x/√(d²+x²). The total restoring force is Fnet = –2F sin θ.

    Fnet=−2q24πε0·x(d2+x2)3/2

  4. Step 4

    Small-displacement approximation

    Since x ≪ d, we have (d²+x²)^{3/2} ≈ d³. The restoring force simplifies to Fnet ≈ –[q²/(2πε₀d³)]x, which is linear in x, proving SHM.

    Fnet≈−q22πε0d3x

  5. Step 5

    Spring constant and angular frequency

    Comparing with F = –mω²x gives the effective spring constant k = q²/(2πε₀d³). Hence ω² = k/m.

    ω2=q22πε0md3

  6. Step 6

    Time period of SHM

    T = 2π/ω. Squaring T² = 4π²/ω² = 4π² × 2πε₀md³/q² = 8π³ε₀md³/q², giving the required result.

    T=[8π3ε0md3q2]1/2

Final answer

The charge +q performs SHM with time period T=[8π3ε0 m d3q2]1/2 The restoring force arises because the net attractive force from the two fixed –q charges has a component anti-parallel to the displacement x, and for x ≪ d this force is proportional to x.
