A non-conducting sheet of large surface area and thickness d contains a uniform charge distribution of density ρ. Find the electric field at a point P inside the plate, at a distance x from the central plane. Draw a qualitative graph of E against x for 0 < x < d.

Step-by-step solution

Let the non-conducting plate occupy −d/2≤z≤d/2, with its central plane at z=0. By symmetry, the electric field inside is perpendicular to the plate and depends only on the distance from the central plane. For a point on the positive side at distance x, where 0<x<d/2, the field is
E→=ρxε0n^
where n^ is directed away from the central plane. Its magnitude is therefore E=ρx/ε0. The graph is a straight line through the origin. Since the stated interval 0<x<d extends beyond the physical interior when d denotes the total thickness, the field is linear only up to x=d/2; outside the plate it remains constant.

  1. Step 1

    Choose a Gaussian surface

    Take a cylindrical Gaussian surface of cross-sectional area A, with its flat faces symmetrically placed at distances +x and −x from the central plane. By planar symmetry, the field is normal to the sheet and has equal magnitude on both faces.

    ΦE=EA+EA=2EA

  2. Step 2

    Calculate the enclosed charge

    The Gaussian cylinder encloses a slab of thickness 2x. Since the volume charge density is uniform and equal to ρ, the enclosed charge is the density multiplied by the enclosed volume.

    qenc=ρ(2xA)

  3. Step 3

    Apply Gauss's law

    Using Gauss's law, the total electric flux equals the enclosed charge divided by ε0.

    2EA=2ρxAε0

  4. Step 4

    Obtain the field inside

    Cancel the common factor 2A. The magnitude of the electric field at distance x from the central plane is proportional to x.

    E(x)=ρxε0,0≤x≤d2

  5. Step 5

    State the direction

    For positive ρ, the field is directed away from the central plane on either side. In vector form, if z is a signed coordinate measured from the central plane, the result is odd in z.

    E→(z)=ρzε0z^,|z|≤d2

  6. Step 6

    Describe the graph

    At the central plane, E=0. As the distance increases inside the plate, the magnitude increases linearly and reaches ρd/(2ε0) at either surface. If the graph is continued outside the plate, the magnitude remains constant there.

    E(d2)=ρd2ε0;E(x)=ρd2ε0forx≥d2

  7. Step 7

    Resolve the interval stated in the question

    If d is the total thickness, the interior range measured from the central plane is 0<x<d/2, not 0<x<d. If the question intends 0<x<d as the interior range, then d must represent the half-thickness; in that convention the same formula applies throughout the stated interval and the surface value is ρd/ε0.

    total thicknessd⇒0<x<d/2

Final answer

For a plate of total thickness d, centred at z=0, the electric field at an interior point a distance x from the central plane is
E→=ρxε0n^,0<x<d2,
where n^ points away from the central plane. Thus E versus x is a straight line starting from E=0 at x=0 and reaching E=ρd/(2ε0) at the surface. Beyond the surface, the field remains constant. The question's range 0<x<d is consistent only if d denotes the half-thickness rather than the total thickness.

Additional study notes

Understand the answer

Place the central plane at z=0, so a plate of total thickness d extends from −d/2 to +d/2. Planar symmetry makes the electric field normal to the plate. At two points equally far from the centre but on opposite sides, its magnitude is equal and its direction is opposite.

Choose a cylindrical Gaussian surface of face area A, with its flat faces at +x and −x. The electric field is parallel to the curved surface, so that part contributes no flux. Each flat face contributes EA, giving total flux 2EA. The cylinder encloses volume 2xA and hence charge 2ρxA. Gauss’s law therefore gives
2EA=2ρxAε0⇒E=ρxε0.
For positive ρ, the field points away from the central plane. Using signed coordinate z, the complete interior result is
E→(z)=ρzε0z^,|z|≤d2.
Thus, on the positive side, E rises linearly from zero at the centre to ρd/(2ε0) at the surface. Outside, it remains constant. Therefore, if d means total thickness, the requested interval 0<x<d includes an exterior part: the graph is linear up to d/2 and horizontal afterwards. If d instead means half-thickness, the straight-line graph continues throughout 0<x<d and reaches ρd/ε0 at the surface.

Reasoning path

  1. Use a Gaussian cylinder centred on the plate, with its two flat faces at equal distances x from the central plane.
  2. By symmetry, the two flat faces have equal outward electric flux, while the curved surface has zero flux. Hence ΦE=2EA.
  3. The enclosed slab has thickness 2x, cross-sectional area A, volume 2xA, and charge qenc=2ρxA.
  4. Apply Gauss’s law: 2EA=2ρxA/ε0. Cancelling 2A gives E=ρx/ε0.
  5. Check the centre: putting x=0 gives E=0, as required because fields from the two halves cancel there.
  6. For total thickness d, substitute x=d/2 to obtain the surface magnitude E=ρd/(2ε0).
  7. Sketch a straight rising line from (0,0) to (d/2,ρd/(2ε0)); if extended to x=d, continue it horizontally after d/2.

Common mistake

Do not treat 0<x<d as entirely inside when d is the total thickness. Distance from the central plane to either surface is only d/2. Also, both flat faces of the Gaussian cylinder contribute flux, so the total is 2EA, not EA.

Exam tip

State clearly whether d denotes total thickness or half-thickness before drawing the graph. Labelling the surface point prevents the interval ambiguity from affecting the final result.
