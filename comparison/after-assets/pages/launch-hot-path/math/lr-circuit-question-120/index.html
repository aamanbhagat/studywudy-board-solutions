An LR circuit with emf ε is connected at t = 0. (a) Find the charge Q which flows through the battery during 0 to t. (b) Calculate the work done by the battery during this period. (c) Find the heat developed during this period. (d) Find the magnetic field energy stored in the circuit at time t. (e) Verify that the results in the three parts above are consistent with energy conservation.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Step 1

    Given

    Consider a series circuit having resistance R, inductance L, and a battery of constant emf ε. The circuit is closed at t=0, so the current initially is zero.

    i(0)=0

  2. Step 2

    Sign convention

    Take current in the direction in which the battery supplies charge as positive. Hence the charge flowing through the battery and the work done by it are positive.

    Q=∫0tidt,WB=εQ

  3. Step 3

    Formula: circuit equation

    Applying Kirchhoff’s loop rule after closing the circuit gives the LR growth equation.

    Ldidt+Ri=ε

  4. Step 4

    Formula: time constant

    Define the time constant of the LR circuit.

    τ=LR

  5. Step 5

    Calculation: current growth

    Solving the differential equation with i(0)=0 gives the current at time t.

    i(t)=εR(1−e−t/τ)=εR(1−e−Rt/L)

  6. Step 6

    Formula: charge through battery

    The charge delivered during 0 to t is the time integral of the current.

    Q=∫0ti(t')dt'

  7. Step 7

    Substitution: charge

    Substitute the current-growth expression, using t' as the integration variable.

    Q=εR∫0t(1−e−t'/τ)dt'

  8. Step 8

    Calculation: charge integral

    Integrating term by term gives the charge that flows through the battery.

    Q=εR[t−τ(1−e−t/τ)]

  9. Step 9

    Final answer (a)

    Thus, the charge supplied by the battery in the interval 0 to t is:

    Q=εR[t−LR(1−e−Rt/L)]C

  10. Step 10

    Formula: work done by battery

    The battery has constant emf ε; therefore, its work equals emf multiplied by the charge supplied.

    WB=εQ

  11. Step 11

    Substitution: battery work

    Substitute the charge found above.

    WB=ε{εR[t−τ(1−e−t/τ)]}

  12. Step 12

    Calculation: battery work

    Simplifying gives the work done by the battery during the interval.

    WB=ε2R[t−τ(1−e−t/τ)]

  13. Step 13

    Final answer (b)

    The work done by the battery is:

    WB=ε2R[t−LR(1−e−Rt/L)]J

  14. Step 14

    Formula: Joule heat

    Heat is developed only in the resistance. The instantaneous rate of heat production is i2R.

    H=∫0ti2(t')Rdt'

  15. Step 15

    Substitution: Joule heat

    Substitute the expression for current.

    H=ε2R∫0t(1−e−t'/τ)2dt'

  16. Step 16

    Calculation: expand integrand

    Expand the square before integration.

    (1−e−t'/τ)2=1−2e−t'/τ+e−2t'/τ

  17. Step 17

    Calculation: heat integral

    Integrating each term over 0 to t gives the heat developed.

    H=ε2R[t−2τ(1−e−t/τ)+τ2(1−e−2t/τ)]

  18. Step 18

    Final answer (c)

    Therefore, the heat developed in the resistor is:

    H=ε2R[t−2LR(1−e−Rt/L)+L2R(1−e−2Rt/L)]J

  19. Step 19

    Formula: magnetic field energy

    The energy stored in the magnetic field of an inductor carrying current i is:

    UB=12Li2

  20. Step 20

    Substitution: magnetic energy

    Substitute the current at time t.

    UB=12L[εR(1−e−t/τ)]2

  21. Step 21

    Final answer (d)

    Hence, the magnetic field energy stored at time t is:

    UB=Lε22R2(1−e−Rt/L)2J

  22. Step 22

    Formula: energy comparison

    For verification, subtract the heat developed from the battery work.

    WB−H=ε2R{τ(1−e−t/τ)−τ2(1−e−2t/τ)}

  23. Step 23

    Calculation: simplify energy difference

    Using 1−e−2t/τ=(1−e−t/τ)(1+e−t/τ), simplify the expression.

    WB−H=ε2τ2R(1−e−t/τ)2

  24. Step 24

    Calculation: identify magnetic energy

    Since τ=L/R, the preceding expression is exactly the inductor’s magnetic energy.

    WB−H=Lε22R2(1−e−t/τ)2=UB

  25. Step 25

    Final answer (e)

    Thus, all energy supplied by the battery is accounted for as Joule heat in the resistor and energy stored in the inductor’s magnetic field.

    WB=H+UB

Final answer

Let τ=L/R. For a series LR circuit switched on at t=0,

Q=εR[t−τ(1−e−t/τ)]C

WB=ε2R[t−τ(1−e−t/τ)]J

H=ε2R[t−2τ(1−e−t/τ)+τ2(1−e−2t/τ)]J

UB=Lε22R2(1−e−t/τ)2J

Moreover, WB−H=UB; hence WB=H+UB, verifying energy conservation.

Additional study notes

Understand the answer

When the switch is closed, the current in the series LR circuit does not jump immediately to its final value because the inductor opposes the growth of current. With τ=L/R, the current is i(t)=εR(1−e−t/τ). This rising current is the starting point for every part of the question.

For charge supplied by the battery, add up the current over the interval, not simply the current at the final instant. Thus Q=∫0ti(t')dt', giving Q=εR[t−τ(1−e−t/τ)]. Its unit is coulomb, since current in ampere multiplied by time in second gives coulomb.

As the emf is constant, battery work is WB=εQ. Therefore it has the same time-dependent bracket as Q, multiplied by another factor of ε. The unit is joule because volt multiplied by coulomb is joule.

The resistor converts electrical energy into heat at the instantaneous rate i2R. Hence heat must be found by integrating i2R, rather than by multiplying R by the square of the current at time t. Substitution of the growing current produces H=ε2R[t−2τ(1−e−t/τ)+τ2(1−e−2t/τ)].

The remaining supplied energy is stored in the inductor’s magnetic field: UB=12Li2. At time t, this becomes Lε22R2(1−e−t/τ)2. Energy conservation is checked algebraically by subtracting heat from battery work. The result is exactly UB, so WB=H+UB. As a useful check, at t=0, charge, work, heat, and stored magnetic energy are all zero. Also, as time becomes large, the current tends to ε/R, while magnetic energy tends to the finite value Lε2/(2R2).

Reasoning path

  1. Use the LR growth equation Ldi/dt+Ri=ε with initial current i(0)=0, and define τ=L/R.
  2. Write the current as i(t)=εR(1−e−t/τ). Integrate it from 0 to t to obtain Q=εR[t−τ(1−e−t/τ)]C.
  3. Because the battery emf is constant, multiply the delivered charge by ε: WB=ε2R[t−τ(1−e−t/τ)]J.
  4. Calculate resistor heating from H=∫0ti2(t')Rdt'. Expanding (1−e−t'/τ)2 gives H=ε2R[t−2τ(1−e−t/τ)+τ2(1−e−2t/τ)]J.
  5. Substitute the current at time t into UB=12Li2, obtaining UB=Lε22R2(1−e−t/τ)2J.
  6. Subtracting the heat expression from the battery-work expression gives WB−H=UB; therefore the supplied energy is completely divided between resistor heat and magnetic-field energy.

Common mistake

Do not use H=i2(t)Rt. The current changes continuously after switching on, so heat requires ∫0ti2(t')Rdt'. Similarly, charge is ∫0ti(t')dt', not i(t)t.

Exam tip

Keep τ=L/R until the final line. It makes the exponential terms and the energy check shorter; replace τ by L/R only if the answer is required entirely in L and R.
