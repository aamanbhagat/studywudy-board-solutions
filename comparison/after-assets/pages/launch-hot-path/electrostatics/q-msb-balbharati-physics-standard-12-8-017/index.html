Step-by-step solution

  1. Given quantities

    List all data extracted from the problem:

    • Number of drops: n = 125
    • Charge per small drop: q = 0.5 µC = 0.5 × 10⁻⁶ C
    • Diameter of each small drop: d = 0.1m, so radius r = 0.05 m
    • Coulomb's constant: k = 9 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻²

  2. Radius of the bigger drop using volume conservation

    When drops merge, total volume is conserved. Setting the volume of the big drop equal to 125 times the volume of a small drop gives R = n1/3 × r.

    R=n1/3r=(125)1/3×0.05=5×0.05=0.25m

  3. Total charge on the bigger drop

    Charge is conserved during merging. The bigger drop carries the sum of all individual charges.

    Q=nq=125×0.5×10−6=62.5×10−6C

  4. Formula for electric potential at the surface

    A charged conducting sphere of radius R and total charge Q behaves like a point charge at its centre for external points. At the surface (distance R from centre), the potential is V = kQ/R.

    V=kQR

  5. Substitution

    Insert the known values into the formula.

    V=9×109×62.5×10−60.25

  6. Calculation

    Evaluate the numerator first: 9 × 10⁹ × 62.5 × 10⁻⁶ = 562 500. Then divide by 0.25 to get 2 250 000 V.

    V=5625000.25=2.25×106V

Final answerThe electric potential at the surface of the bigger drop is V = 2.25 × 10⁶ V (2.25 MV).
