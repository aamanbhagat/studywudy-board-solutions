A 6 μF capacitor is charged by a 300 V supply. It is then disconnected from the supply and is connected to another uncharged 3 μF capacitor. How much electrostatic energy of the first capacitor is lost in the form of heat and electromagnetic radiation?

Step-by-step solution

Step-by-step solution

  1. Step 1

    Initial charge on C₁

    The charge stored on the first capacitor before disconnection is found from Q = CV.

    Q=C1V=6×10−6×300=1.8×10−3C

  2. Step 2

    Initial electrostatic energy of C₁

    The energy stored in a capacitor charged to voltage V is U = ½CV². Since only C₁ is charged initially, this is the total initial energy.

    Ui=12C1V2=12×6×10−6×(300)2=0.27J

  3. Step 3

    Common potential after connecting C₂

    When C₁ (charged) is connected to C₂ (uncharged), charge distributes until both reach the same potential. By conservation of charge, Qtotal = Q; total capacitance = C₁ + C₂.

    V'=QC1+C2=1.8×10−39×10−6=200V

  4. Step 4

    Final total electrostatic energy

    After redistribution, both capacitors are at V' = 200 V. The total final energy is computed using the combined capacitance.

    Uf=12(C1+C2)V'2=12×9×10−6×(200)2=0.18J

  5. Step 5

    Energy lost as heat and electromagnetic radiation

    The difference between initial and final stored energy is dissipated as heat (in connecting wires/resistance) and electromagnetic radiation. Since C₂ was uncharged, all initial energy was in C₁.

    ΔU=Ui−Uf=0.27−0.18=0.09J

  6. Step 6

    Verification using direct formula

    The energy loss for two capacitors can be written compactly as ΔU = C₁C₂V²/[2(C₁+C₂)], confirming the result.

    ΔU=C1C22(C1+C2)V2=6×10−6×3×10−62×9×10−6×(300)2=1×10−6×90000=0.09J

Final answer

The electrostatic energy lost from the first capacitor (dissipated as heat and electromagnetic radiation) is ΔU = 0.09J.
