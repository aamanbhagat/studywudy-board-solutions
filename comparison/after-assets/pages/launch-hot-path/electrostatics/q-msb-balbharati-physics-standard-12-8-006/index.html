Question 6 · Numerical

Question

Answer in brief:

A charge q is moved from a point A above a dipole of dipole moment p to a point B below the dipole in an equatorial plane without acceleration. Find the work done in this process.

Solution

Answer and explanation

Step-by-step solution

  1. Step 1

    Identify the geometry

    From the figure, the dipole of moment p lies along the horizontal axis (from −q to +q). Point A is directly above the centre and point B is directly below the centre — both lie on the perpendicular bisector of the dipole axis, which is the equatorial plane of the dipole.

  2. Step 2

    Electric potential on the equatorial plane

    For a point P on the equatorial plane at distance r from the centre of the dipole, the distances from +q and −q are equal. Their potentials are equal in magnitude but opposite in sign and cancel exactly.

    Vequatorial=14πε0pcos90°r2=0

  3. Step 3

    Potential at A and B

    Since both A and B are on the equatorial plane, the electric potential at each point is zero regardless of the distance from the centre.

    VA=0, VB=0

  4. Step 4

    Work–energy theorem (no acceleration)

    Work done by an external agent in moving charge q from A to B without acceleration equals the change in electric potential energy.

    W=q(VB−VA)

  5. Step 5

    Substitution and calculation

    Substituting the values of VA and VB into the work formula.

    W=q(0−0)=0

Final answer

The work done in moving charge q from point A to point B along the equatorial plane of the dipole is W = 0 (zero joules). The potential is zero everywhere on the equatorial plane, so there is no potential difference and hence no work is done.
