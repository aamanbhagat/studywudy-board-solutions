Answer in brief:

A spherical shell of radius b with charge Q is expanded to a radius a. Find the work done by the electrical forces in the process.

Main solution
  • ✓ 6 worked steps
  • ✓ Formula or principle identified
  • ✓ Final answer separated

Step-by-step solution

Answer

The work done by electrical forces is W=Q28πε0(1b−1a)

Since a>b, this quantity is positive, confirming that the mutually repulsive charges on the shell do positive work as the shell expands.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Step 1

    Identify the governing principle

    The work done by electrical (conservative) forces equals the decrease in the electrical potential energy of the system: W=Ui−Uf.

    W=Ui−Uf

  2. Step 2

    Potential energy of a uniformly charged spherical shell

    For a spherical shell of radius r carrying total charge Q, the electrostatic self-energy (energy stored in the surrounding electric field) is obtained by integrating 12ε0E2 over all space outside the shell, giving:

    U=Q28πε0r

  3. Step 3

    Initial potential energy (radius b)

    Before expansion the shell has radius b, so its self-energy is:

    Ui=Q28πε0b

  4. Step 4

    Final potential energy (radius a)

    After expansion the shell has radius a (with a>b), so its self-energy is:

    Uf=Q28πε0a

  5. Step 5

    Calculate the work done

    Applying W=Ui−Uf:

    W=Q28πε0b−Q28πε0a=Q28πε0(1b−1a)

  6. Step 6

    Physical interpretation

    Because a>b, we have 1b>1a, so W>0. The repulsive Coulomb forces between like charges on the shell drive the expansion, doing positive work and lowering the stored field energy.

Final answer

Work done by electrical forces: W=Q28πε0(1b−1a) The result is positive (for a>b), because the repulsive electrical forces aid the expansion.
